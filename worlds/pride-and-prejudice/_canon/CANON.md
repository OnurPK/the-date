# Pride & Prejudice — canon reference for roles.ai

Source text: `pride-and-prejudice.txt` in this folder (Jane Austen, 1813, public domain; 61 chapters, `## Chapter N` headings — grep it for any fact or phrase before you assert it). Chapter numbers below refer to that file.

## 1. Deliberate deviations (licensed — do NOT "correct" these)

- **The player, Arabella Frost, replaces Elizabeth Bennet** as the outsider in the Darcy duel. Elizabeth may still exist as an NPC (she does in `characters/elizabeth_bennet`), but Darcy's story turns on Arabella.
- **Invented suitors** with no canon source: Lord Sebastian Ravenscar, Sir Henry Ashbourne, Captain Frederick Vane, Monsieur Rafael Devereux, Mr Augustus Hale, Mr Thomas Fenwick, Ensign Edward Pryce, Mr Jasper Quill (+ minor: Mr Frost, Mrs Frost, Julian Vane, Miss Vere, Mr Ashford, Mr Coyle, the maid). Their houses (Ravenscar Hall, Ashbourne Court, the Anchorage, Hale House, Fenwick Cottage, the Garret…) are invented.
- **Compressed geography/time:** Pemberley, Rosings, Brighton, Hunsford and Meryton sit on one map and can be visited in any order; the novel's calendar (autumn → the following autumn) is replaced by the game's turn clock.
- **Episode order is not the novel's order.** The Netherfield ball (canon ch. 18) is episode 1; Sir Ashbourne calls at Longbourn (invented) is episode 2.
- Canon characters keep their canon facts (below). Anything else about them may be invented as long as it does not contradict the book.

## 2. Canon characters — facts that must hold

**Fitzwilliam Darcy** — of Pemberley, Derbyshire; ten thousand a year (ch. 3); tall, handsome, proud, "above being pleased" at the Meryton assembly (ch. 3); sister Georgiana, "about fifteen or sixteen" per Wickham (ch. 16), fortune thirty thousand pounds (ch. 35), nearly eloped with Wickham at Ramsgate (ch. 35); aunt Lady Catherine de Bourgh of Rosings; cousin Colonel Fitzwilliam, younger son of an earl (ch. 30); father the late Mr Darcy, Wickham's godfather; housekeeper Mrs Reynolds (ch. 43). Dances only with Bingley's sisters at the assembly; refuses Elizabeth ("tolerable, but not handsome enough to tempt me", ch. 3). First proposal at Hunsford, ch. 34; letter ch. 35; second proposal ch. 58.

**Charles Bingley** — leases Netherfield Park, Hertfordshire, before Michaelmas (ch. 1); "four or five thousand a year" (ch. 1), inherited nearly a hundred thousand pounds from a father in trade in the north (ch. 4); amiable, easily led; sisters Caroline (Miss Bingley) and Louisa (Mrs Hurst); brother-in-law Mr Hurst "merely looked the gentleman" (ch. 3). Gives the Netherfield ball (ch. 18). Leaves for London after it (ch. 21); returns ch. 53; engaged to Jane ch. 55.

**George Wickham** — son of old Mr Darcy's steward; lieutenant in the militia regiment quartered at Meryton for the winter (ch. 7, 15); handsome, "happy readiness of conversation" (ch. 15); claims Darcy cheated him of the Kympton living; actually took three thousand pounds in lieu of it and a legacy of one thousand (ch. 35); pursued Miss King's ten thousand pounds (ch. 26); elopes with Lydia from Brighton (ch. 46); marriage bought by Darcy (ch. 52).

**William Collins** — clergyman, rector of Hunsford near Westerham, Kent, under Lady Catherine's patronage (ch. 13); heir to Longbourn by entail; proposes to Elizabeth (ch. 19), then marries Charlotte Lucas (ch. 22); pompous, obsequious, quotes his patroness constantly.

**Caroline Bingley** — fashionable, twenty thousand pounds (ch. 4), pursues Darcy, sneers at the Bennets; "fine eyes" jealousy (ch. 6).

**Jane Bennet** — eldest, 22-ish, beautiful, gentle, thinks well of everyone; falls ill at Netherfield (ch. 7).
**Elizabeth Bennet** — second daughter, "not one-and-twenty" (ch. 29); the novel's heroine (see deviations).
**Mary** — plain, moralising, reads and makes extracts (ch. 2). **Kitty** — coughs, follows Lydia. **Lydia** — youngest, fifteen, "stout, well-grown", flirts with officers, elopes (ch. 46).
**Mr Bennet** — dry, ironic, retreats to his library; estate Longbourn, two thousand a year, entailed on Collins (ch. 7).
**Mrs Bennet** — "a woman of mean understanding, little information, and uncertain temper"; her business is to get her daughters married (ch. 1); nerves.
**Charlotte Lucas** — "about twenty-seven" (ch. 5), plain, sensible, Elizabeth's friend; marries Collins for an establishment (ch. 22). Father **Sir William Lucas**, knighted, formerly in trade at Meryton (ch. 5); Lucas Lodge.
**Lady Catherine de Bourgh** — widow of Sir Lewis de Bourgh; Rosings Park, Kent; daughter Anne, sickly, "intended" for Darcy (ch. 29). Visits Longbourn to forbid the match (ch. 56).
**Mr and Mrs Gardiner** — Mrs Bennet's brother (in trade, Gracechurch Street, London) and his wife, formerly of Lambton; take Elizabeth to Derbyshire (ch. 42–43).
**Colonel Forster** — commands the militia; his wife Harriet invites Lydia to Brighton (ch. 41).
**Mrs Philips** — Mrs Bennet's sister, Meryton.

## 5. Money, rank, manners (period rules)

- Incomes are given "a year"; fortunes as lump sums ("twenty thousand pounds") that yield ~4–5 %. £10,000/yr = very great; £2,000/yr = gentleman of modest estate; a curate ≈ £50–100.
- Address: "Mr Darcy", "Miss Bennet" (eldest), "Miss Elizabeth"; first names only within family or engagement. "Sir William" (knight/baronet by first name), "Lady Catherine" (earl's daughter: Lady + first name), "Lady Lucas" (wife of a knight: Lady + surname).
- Militia officers: colonel, captain, lieutenant, ensign; red coats ("regimentals"). Navy is a separate service.
- Dancing: a "set" of two dances; being asked twice is notable; a lady cannot ask; refusing one partner means sitting out.
- Calls, cards, carriages (chaise and four, curricle, barouche); letters by post or express; entail; living (church post in a patron's gift); "coming out"; the assembly (public ball) vs a private ball.
- Vocabulary that fits: agreeable, amiable, civility, condescension (a virtue in a superior), consequence, countenance, establishment, felicity, impertinent, obliging, sensible (= aware), tolerable, vexed, want (= lack). Avoid: okay, nice (in the modern sense), guys, focus, relationship, boyfriend, date.

- Mrs Bennet: "You have no compassion for my poor nerves." (ch. 1)
- Mr Bennet: "For what do we live, but to make sport for our neighbours, and laugh at them in our turn?" (ch. 57)
- Charlotte: "Happiness in marriage is entirely a matter of chance." (ch. 5)
- Lady Catherine: "Are the shades of Pemberley to be thus polluted?" (ch. 56)
