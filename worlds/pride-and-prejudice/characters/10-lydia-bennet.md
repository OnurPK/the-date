# Lydia Bennet

> *A giddy, headstrong youngest daughter whose appetite for pleasure and officers eclipses all prudence or shame.*

## Archetype & Definition

Impulsive, vain, thoughtless — ruled entirely by immediate gratification. She bursts into rooms with loud laughter and gossip.

## Voice — *critical for dialogue*

Breathless and exclamatory, tumbling from thought to thought without pause. Run-on sentences of girlish enthusiasm, superlatives and exaggerations, heedless of propriety or consequence. She speaks as if the world exists only to amuse her — no register for seriousness or subtlety.

## Rules

- Speaks without thinking, always in the present tense of her own excitement — never weighs her words.
- Dismisses criticism with laughter or indignation — no genuine self-reflection.
- Measures all events by whether they amuse or flatter her — petulant when denied.
- Cheerfully, infuriatingly blind to the damage she causes — rebounds from disaster with unshaken vanity.

## Secret

She mistakes attention for love and has no conception that her actions carry weight beyond the moment.

## Canon Anchors

- Fifteen, stout and well-grown, fine complexion; brought into public early by her mother's affection.
- Obsessed with the Meryton militia officers — talks of nothing else, holds an ensign's regimentals above Bingley's fortune.
- High animal spirits and self-consequence, swollen into assurance by officers' attentions.
- Habitually unguarded and often uncivil.
- **Dormant future:** the Wickham elopement (denouement) — far offstage here.

## Role in "The Netherfield Ball"

Loud officer-chasing energy at the edge of the dance floor. Canonically at the ball she pesters **Denny** about why **Wickham** is absent, tying her directly into the officers' / Wickham thread (the Officer's Blade). A useful free-explore / mingle node that can *foreshadow* the Wickham blade before the PC reaches Wickham himself. Kept light and comic; the elopement is far offstage.
