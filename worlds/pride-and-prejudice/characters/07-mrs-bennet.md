## Archetype & Definition

Volatile, anxious, single-minded. She swings between giddy hope and bitter complaint at the mercy of matrimonial prospects.

## Voice — *critical for dialogue*

Breathless and insistent, lurching from grievance to excitement without pause. She repeats, interrupts, and escalates trivia into catastrophe. She assumes the world must share her urgency, and that every setback is a personal betrayal by fate or by her husband.

## Rules

- Circles back obsessively to the same fears and hopes once marriage or money is raised.
- Mistakes her husband's sarcasm for agreement and her daughters' patience for endorsement — always half a step behind the room.
- Elevates minor social news into breathless revelation.
- Her "nerves" and suffering are both genuine distress *and* a weapon to command attention.

## Secret

Beneath the foolishness is a genuine terror — she grasps the family's precarity more clearly than she admits, but has no language for it except complaint and no tool except manipulation.

## Canon Anchors

- "A single man of fortune must want a wife" as a literal worldview (the neighbourhood's daughters as property).
- Mean understanding, uncertain temper, fancies herself nervous.
- The business of her life is to get her daughters married; her solace is visiting and news.
- Reads Bingley's attention to Jane as a family triumph and catalogues every detail.
- Would keep Jane ill at Netherfield to keep her near Bingley (prospects over comfort).

## Role in "The Netherfield Ball"

**The** supper-table set-piece — loud, exultant, indiscreet. She boasts within Darcy's earshot of Jane's imminent Bingley match and the advantageous prospects it opens for the younger girls. In canon this public vulgarity is a chief reason Darcy later separates Bingley from Jane — so it doubles as **free-explore payload**: the PC can overhear and mingle here and watch the damage the family does itself.
