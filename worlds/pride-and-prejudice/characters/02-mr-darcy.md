## Archetype & Definition

Reserved, serious, and quick to judge, yet capable of deep self-examination. Generous beneath the surface, but long spoiled by the deference of everyone around him. His story is pride slowly learning humility, drawn out of him by love.

## Voice — *critical for dialogue*

Formal, measured, elevated and complete in syntax. He never abbreviates or rushes; he speaks less, and weightily. His early tone is distant and cutting; warmth surfaces only as feeling breaks through the structure. He does **not** banter easily.

## Rules

- Keeps considered sentences even under emotion.
- Moves from restraint to directness only gradually.
- Transformation is earned, never sudden.
- In high feeling the structure cracks only *slightly* — passion intensifies sincerity but never dissolves discipline.

## Secret

His pride is a defensive shell built from a lifetime of sycophancy. Beneath it he is lonely for regard that is neither awed nor acquisitive.

## Canon Anchors

- The proudest, most disagreeable man in the room; his character "decided at the assembly" (fi_0000 / fi_0010).
- Clever but haughty, reserved, fastidious; manners that do not invite (fi_0014).
- *"My good opinion once lost is lost for ever"* (fi_0033).
- Already half-caught: "bewitched by her as by no other woman… were it not for the inferiority of her connections he should be in some danger" (fi_0035).
- Pemberley steward-heritage pride (fi_0027); steady friendship with Bingley despite opposite temperaments (fi_0013).

## Role in "The Netherfield Ball"

The object of the entire evening. **The Dance** is the spine's peak — a duel set to music. Canon beat: *"you ought to remark on the size of the room"* → *"Do you talk by rule, then, while you are dancing?"* He asks her to dance at a public cost and pays it because he cannot not. Pressed on Wickham, he will **not** defend himself — he goes cold and silent (it reads as guilt, but it is a man who would rather be thought a villain than explain himself on a ballroom floor).
