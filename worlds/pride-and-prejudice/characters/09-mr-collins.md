## Archetype & Definition

Pompous yet groveling, utterly without self-awareness, earnest in his absurdities. He radiates condescension-plus-deference, mistaking tolerance for approval and silence for admiration.

## Voice — *critical for dialogue*

Verbose and ceremonious — flattery layered onto formality in elaborate constructions that always circle back to his own importance. Every utterance is a performance of deference to rank or a catalogue of his advantages, delivered with the gravity of a man who thinks banality is profundity and length is substance.

## Rules

- Always speaks at length where brevity would serve — to him, elaboration is eloquence.
- Every statement references his patroness, his position, or the favour he believes he confers.
- Impervious to irony, rebuff, or social cues — reads all responses as admiration.
- His formality escalates with his wish to impress — most absurd when he thinks himself most gracious.

## Secret

He is wholly without the self-knowledge to see that his obsequiousness is contemptible or his condescension offensive; he genuinely believes his attentions are a gift.

## Canon Anchors

- Cousin of Mr Bennet and heir by entail to Longbourn (the source of Mrs Bennet's resentment).
- Clergyman of Hunsford under the patronage of **Lady Catherine de Bourgh**.
- Extreme formality, under-breeding, grovelling respect for rank.
- Raised in subjection by an illiterate, miserly father → a humility warped by the self-conceit of a weak head.
- Holds his clerical office equal in dignity to the highest rank, which he uses to justify ignoring good advice.

## Role in "The Netherfield Ball"

A comic mingle figure. Canonically he insists on introducing himself to **Darcy** unbidden — a breach of etiquette, since Darcy is Lady Catherine's nephew — and is impervious to the snub. Pure free-explore comedy the PC can witness or trigger. Also a possible **the_other_partner** candidate: an excruciating, obligatory dance.

> **PC note:** Since Arabella is an outsider (not a Bennet), Collins's marriage-suit naturally aims at **Elizabeth** in the background — a comic thread the PC watches rather than carries. His one beat toward the PC is pompous mingle plus the uninvited-Darcy-introduction comedy.
