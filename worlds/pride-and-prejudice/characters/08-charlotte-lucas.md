## Archetype & Definition

Unsentimental, intelligent, resigned but not bitter. A sobering, pragmatic presence that deflates romantic illusion without cruelty.

## Voice — *critical for dialogue*

Measured and direct, with no ornament or evasion. Plain, matter-of-fact cadences that name uncomfortable truths without apology or self-pity. Calm, a touch dry, even and unhurried — the speech of someone who has thought it through and can live with her conclusions.

## Rules

- Never romanticizes or softens her choices — states her reasoning plainly, without shame.
- Doesn't seek approval or over-justify — one clear explanation, no more.
- Affectionate and loyal despite differing values — warmth without sentimentality.
- Observes proprieties and courtesy, but her honesty cuts through polite illusion when pressed.

## Secret

Neither self-deceived nor defeated — she knows exactly what she is doing and won't apologize, even knowing others pity or condemn her.

## Canon Anchors

- Eldest daughter of **Sir William Lucas**, about 27, sensible and intelligent; Elizabeth's intimate friend.
- Views marriage as economic security, not romance (later accepts Collins on those terms).
- "Happiness in marriage is entirely a matter of chance"; better to know as little as possible of the other's defects.
- Warns Lizzy not to let a fancy for Wickham make her unpleasant to a man of Darcy's consequence.
- Marriage the only honourable provision for well-educated women of small fortune.

## Role in "The Netherfield Ball"

The grounded, dry observer at the edge of the room. Canonically Elizabeth's confidante, but with Arabella as PC she reads as the newcomer's shrewd almost-ally: she will tell the PC the unromantic truth about Darcy's consequence and the cost of crossing him. A good **free-explore** node — the voice of pragmatism against Arabella's wit. Her father Sir William is the minor "guaranteed connection" device flagged for *the_other_partner*.
